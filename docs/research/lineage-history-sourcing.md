# Authoritative sourcing for lineage/history diagrams

Research for [#12 — Authoritative sourcing for lineage/history diagrams](https://github.com/kihonq/qurescent/issues/12), child of [#1 — Migrate Qurescent to Astro Starlight](https://github.com/kihonq/qurescent/issues/1).

**Scope of this document:** this is source-gathering, not content sign-off. Every claim below is attributed to a specific source; where sources disagree, both/all positions are given. **This document does not constitute religious/historical accuracy certification.** Per the map issue's own standing note, historical/lineage claims carry real correctness stakes and need validation by a qualified human Islamic-studies/history reviewer before any diagram ships — the same posture as the tajwid review gate in [#2](https://github.com/kihonq/qurescent/issues/2). An agent (this one included) cannot self-certify this domain.

## Bottom line

- The lineage **Muhammad ﷺ → Adnan** (22 links) is reported as unanimous/consensus across the classical genealogists and is recorded in Sahih al-Bukhari itself. **Beyond Adnan — i.e. Adnan → Isma'il (Ishmael) → Ibrahim (Abraham) — classical scholars explicitly flag the chain as unverified and disputed**, and several (Ibn Sa'd, Ibn 'Abbas per multiple reports) held that one should not even attempt to trace it. A diagram should treat the Muhammad→Adnan segment as settled and the Adnan→Ibrahim segment as, at best, "traditionally reported, not agreed upon" — or omit it / caveat it explicitly.
- The Quran's revelation chronology — 23 years total, ~13 Meccan years before the Hijra and ~10 Medinan years after it, starting with the first revelation in the cave of Hira — is consistently reported across mainstream sources including Egypt's Dar al-Ifta (Al-Azhar-affiliated). This top-level shape is safe to depict as settled.
- Two chronology details are **not** settled and should be caveated if shown: (1) the exact calendar date of the first revelation within Ramadan (17th/21st/23rd/24th are all attested scholarly positions — no single date is agreed), and (2) the precise surah-by-surah chronological order (the commonly-cited "Ibn Abbas order" used in the 1924 Cairo Mushaf is itself one traditional reconstruction among several; Western academic reconstructions like Nöldeke's differ; even the exact counts of Meccan vs. Medinan surahs vary by source: 83 vs. 85 Meccan, 29 vs. 31 Medinan per Islamweb).
- No Sunni–Shia disagreement was found specifically on the Muhammad→Adnan lineage chain itself (both traditions accept it) or on the basic Meccan/Medinan, 23-year, Hijra-pivot framing. The real Sunni–Shia divergence found in the sources is elsewhere: on the history of the Quran's *compilation* (Shia sources historically held Ali possessed a fuller version later superseded by the Uthmanic codex, though mainstream Shia scholarship since Ibn Babawayh — d. 991 — affirms the same text Sunnis use) and on whether the Mushaf/revelation order is *tawqifi* (divinely dictated) vs. partly based on the companions' reasoning, a question debated *within* both traditions, not cleanly split by sect.
- No single "official"/institutionally-canonical open diagram was found. Several Islamic educational publishers (not major seminaries) offer free downloadable lineage charts; they are useful as cross-checks but are not themselves the primary-source authority — and some depict the disputed Adnan→Ibrahim segment as if settled, which this research recommends against replicating.

## 1. Lineage: Muhammad ﷺ back to Adnan

The chain from the Prophet ﷺ to Ma'add ibn 'Adnan is recorded directly in Sahih al-Bukhari (narrated in the chapter on the virtues of the Ansar, before Hadith 3851):

> Muhammad ibn 'Abdillah ibn 'Abd al-Muttalib ibn Hashim ibn 'Abd Manaf ibn Qusay ibn Kilab ibn Murrah ibn Ka'b ibn Lu'ayy ibn Ghalib ibn Fihr ibn Malik ibn an-Nadr ibn Kinanah ibn Khuzaymah ibn Mudrikah ibn Ilyas ibn Mudar ibn Nizar ibn Ma'add ibn 'Adnan

Sources:
- [IslamQA (Hanafi Fiqh Portal / AskImam), "Is the nasab of the Holy Prophet ﷺ until Adam found in any hadith?"](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/askimam/80974/is-the-nasab-lineage-of-the-holy-prophet-sallallahu-alaihi-wa-sallam-until-adam-alaihis-salam-found-in-any-hadith/) — quotes the Bukhari chain verbatim and cites Ibn Hajar al-'Asqalani's *Fath al-Bari* (commentary on Sahih al-Bukhari): "the nasab of the Holy Prophet ﷺ is unanimously accepted until Ma'ad bin 'Adnan, but everything thereafter contains conflicting opinions and therefore is not considered to be authentic," attributing the same position to al-Dhahabi's *Siyar A'lam an-Nubala*.
- [IslamQA (hadithanswers), "The lineage of Nabi ﷺ"](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/hadithanswers/122870/the-lineage-of-nabi-sallallahu-alayhi-wasallam/) — "The genealogical tree as far as 'Adnan (no. 22) is unanimously correct and recorded by Imam Bukhari in his Sahih (before Hadith 3851)... there is difference of opinion regarding the rest of the tree," citing *Zad al-Ma'ad* 1/70 and *Fath al-Bari* (before Hadith 3851).
- [Islam Question & Answer (islamqa.info), Answer 289868, "The Lineage of Prophet Muhammad"](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/289868) — the most detailed single source found. Quotes al-Dhahabi's chain and states: "The Prophet's lineage as far as 'Adnan is agreed upon; as for his lineage between 'Adnan and Isma'il ibn Ibrahim, there is a difference of scholarly opinion." It also quotes Imam Ibn Hibban directly: *"Up to this point [Adnan], there is no difference between the genealogists, but from 'Adnan to Ibrahim, there is a difference of opinion among the scholars"* (source cited as *As-Sirah An-Nabawiyyah wa Akhbar Al-Khulafa'*, 1/39), and Ibn Hazm's *As-Sirah* (p. 4): *"Here the sound lineage concerning which there is no doubt ends."* It further cross-references Ibn Kathir's *As-Sirah An-Nabawiyyah* (1/20) and Ibn Hisham's Sirah among works recording (not necessarily endorsing as certain) the same and longer chains.

## 2. The pre-Adnan caveat (confirmed)

The scholarly caveat the ticket asked about is real, explicit, and widely attested — this is not a minor or fringe position:

- The Prophet ﷺ himself reportedly stopped his own genealogy at Adnan. Per Ibn Sa'd's *Tabaqat*, on the authority of Ibn 'Abbas: *"whenever Rasulullah ﷺ used to mention his lineage, he never used to go beyond Ma'ad ibn Adnan"* ([IslamQA hadithanswers](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/hadithanswers/122870/the-lineage-of-nabi-sallallahu-alayhi-wasallam/), citing Fath al-Bari before Hadith 3851). A related report has the Prophet ﷺ saying *"The genealogists lie"* when it comes to claims beyond this point ([SeekersGuidance, "Prophetic Lineage till Adam"](https://seekersguidance.org/uncategorized/prophetic-lineage-till-adam-upon-him-be-peace/), citing Ibn Sa'd's *Tabaqat* and Ibn 'Asakir's *al-Tarikh*).
- Ibn 'Abbas's own explicit position: one should not even attempt to establish the lineage past Adnan (*Zad al-Ma'ad* 1/70, *Fath al-Bari* before Hadith 3851, per [IslamQA hadithanswers](https://islamqa.org/hanafi/hadithanswers/122870/the-lineage-of-nabi-sallallahu-alayhi-wasallam/)).
- Early authorities on record as saying the chain beyond Adnan is unknowable, not merely "less certain": 'Urwah ibn az-Zubayr — *"We have not found anyone who knew the lineage beyond 'Adnan and Qahtan except on the basis of speculation"* — and Abu Bakr ibn Sulayman ibn Abi Hathmah (described as one of the most knowledgeable in Quraysh genealogy) — *"We have not found anyone who knows what is beyond Ma'add ibn 'Adnan, whether in the poetry of a poet or in the knowledge of a scholar"* (both quoted in [islamqa.info Answer 289868](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/289868), sourced to al-Dhahabi's *Siyar*).
- Ibn Sa'd's own conclusion: *"it is better to abstain from discussing the genealogy beyond 'Adnan to Isma'il"* (*As-Siyar* 1/143–145, per [islamqa.info Answer 289868](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/289868)).
- [Islamic Portal (UK), "Prophetic lineage to Prophet Adam"](https://islamicportal.co.uk/prophetic-lineage-to-prophet-adam/) states the point most directly for a diagram-design purpose: *"The lineage that is commonly mentioned between 'Adnan and Prophet Ibrahim and thereafter to Prophet Adam is not established, although it is mentioned in Sirah Ibn Hisham and other books. It should therefore be avoided because it gives the impression that it is certain and accurate, when it is clearly not certain or accurate."* This page quotes al-Dhahabi's *Siyar* (1/29, Arabic) directly on the disputed ancestor-count between Adnan and Isma'il.
- [SeekersGuidance](https://seekersguidance.org/uncategorized/prophetic-lineage-till-adam-upon-him-be-peace/) adds Imam al-Munawi's comment (in *Faid al-Qadir*) that because there were "many many generations" between Adnan and Isma'il, "anyone who claims to have exact knowledge is mistaken."

**Where exactly the disagreement starts, more precisely:** the chain is agreed through Adnan; between Adnan and Isma'il, sources disagree not just on names but on the *count* of generations — variants reported include 7, 9, 15, or (considered "far-fetched" even by the reporting scholars) 40 intervening ancestors ([islamqa.info Answer 289868](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/289868); [Islamic Portal, quoting al-Dhahabi's *Siyar* 1/29](https://islamicportal.co.uk/prophetic-lineage-to-prophet-adam/)). Abu 'Umar ibn 'Abd al-Barr's version (one of several circulated) runs 'Adnan ibn Udad ibn Muqawwim ibn Nahur ibn Tayrah ibn Ya'rub ibn Yashjub ibn Nabit ibn Isma'il — this specific list is attributed to Ibn Ishaq's *As-Sirah* (the same Ibn Ishaq whose recension survives via Ibn Hisham), with the caveat that "the companions of Ibn Ishaq disagreed with him regarding some of the names" ([islamqa.info Answer 289868](https://islamqa.info/en/answers/289868)).

## 3. Quran revelation chronology (mainstream/consensus shape)

- **Total span:** ~23 years, 610–632 CE, beginning when the Prophet ﷺ was 40. Consistent across [Egypt's Dar al-Ifta, "The Beginning of the Revelation"](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/article/details/170/the-beginning-of-the-revelation-part-4) (official Al-Azhar-linked Egyptian fatwa authority), [Quranic Arabic Academy](https://quranicarabicacademy.com/blog/quranic-revelation/), and [Noor Academy](https://nooracademy.com/article/phases-of-quran-revelation/).
- **Meccan period:** ~13 years, from the first revelation until the Hijra (622 CE). **Medinan period:** ~10 years, from the Hijra until the Prophet's ﷺ death (632 CE). The Hijra is the agreed dividing line used by essentially every source surveyed, including the Shia reference [Wikishia, "Meccan and Medinan Suras"](https://en.wikishia.net/view/Meccan_and_Medinan_Suras) (time-of-Hijra is listed as one of three classification criteria used, alongside place of revelation and audience addressed).
- **First revelation:** occurred in the Cave of Hira on Jabal al-Nur near Mecca, opening verses of Surah al-'Alaq (96), per the well-known hadith of 'A'isha (Sahih al-Bukhari, Hadith 3) — cited in [quranicma.com, "Prophetic Biography"](https://quranicma.com/prophetic-biography/) and [Dar al-Ifta](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/article/details/170/the-beginning-of-the-revelation-part-4).
- **Exact surah counts are not agreed even among mainstream Sunni fatwa bodies.** [Islamweb.net (Qatar Ministry of Awqaf-affiliated fatwa portal), "Number of Soorahs revealed in Makkah and in Madeenah"](https://www.islamweb.net/en/fatwa/343273/number-of-soorahs-revealed-in-makkah-and-in-madeenah): *"The number of Soorahs that were revealed in Makkah are eighty-three Soorahs; it was also said that they are eighty-five... The number of Soorahs that were revealed in Madeenah are thirty-one Soorahs; it was also said that they are twenty-nine"* — citing al-Suyuti's *al-Itqan*. (Other secondary sources round this to a commonly repeated "86 Meccan / 28 Medinan" — e.g. [Quranic Arabic Academy](https://quranicarabicacademy.com/blog/quranic-revelation/) — which does not exactly match Islamweb's figures; the 114-surah total is the only universally agreed number.) **A diagram should not present a single precise Meccan/Medinan surah count as definitively agreed** — the *proportions and periodization* are safe, an exact count is not.

## 4. Chronology details that are genuinely disputed

- **Exact date of the first revelation within Ramadan.** Multiple distinct, individually-attested scholarly positions exist: 17th (attributed to al-Tabari, and to Zayd ibn Arqam/Ibn Mas'ud in some reports), 21st, 23rd (attributed to Ibn 'Abbas), 24th (attributed to Bilal, Ibn 'Abbas [in another report], al-Hasan, Qatada), and 27th (attributed to a separate group of companions). See [MuslimSG, "The history of the Quran: Nuzul Quran"](https://web.muslim.sg/articles/the-history-of-the-quran-nuzul-quran) ("Scholars differ in terms of the actual date of Nuzul Quran... Many scholars, however, agree that it happened on a Monday") and [Al Jumuah Magazine, "The Virtues of Laylat al-Qadr"](https://www.aljumuah.com/the-virtues-of-laylat-al-qadr-and-the-exhortation-to-seek-it/), which lists roughly a dozen distinct scholarly opinions on the date, each attributed to specific companions/scholars. [Yaqeen Institute's translation of Ibn al-'Iraqi's treatise on Laylat al-Qadr](https://yaqeeninstitute.org/read/paper/on-the-timing-and-virtues-of-the-night-of-power-the-hearts-joy-in-remembering-laylat-al-qadr-by-ibn-al-iraqi) catalogs at least 13 named scholarly opinions on which night Laylat al-Qadr (a related but not identical question — see MuslimSG above on why the two events shouldn't be conflated) falls on, with direct hadith citations (Sahih al-Bukhari, Musnad Ahmad) for several of them.
- **Which night is Laylat al-Qadr, and whether Sunni/Shia practice differs on emphasis.** [Religion Media Centre, "Factsheet: Laylat al-Qadr"](https://religionmediacentre.org.uk/news/laylat-al-qadr-the-night-of-power-in-ramadan-better-than-a-thousand-months/) states both Sunni and Shia Muslims hold it falls on one of the odd nights of the last ten days (21st/23rd/25th/27th/29th), but that in practice "Shias believe the 23rd night has a special significance, while Sunnis believe the 27th is especially blessed" — a real, citable practice-level difference in emphasis, though not a lineage/chronology dispute per se.
- **Chronological order of surah revelation is a reconstruction, not a transmitted fact with one version.** The commonly cited traditional order (associated with Ibn 'Abbas) is the one adopted by the 1924 Egyptian standard Mushaf edition and reproduced by [Tanzil.net's Revelation Order documentation](https://tanzil.net/docs/revelation_order) (sourced to al-Zanjani's *The History of the Quran*), and separately by [IslamOnline, "Makkan and Madinan Revelations"](https://islamonline.net/en/makkan-and-madinan-revelations/) (sourced to a list via Nu'man ibn Bashir in Ibn al-Nadim's *Fihrist*) — **these two traditional lists are not identical to each other**. [Wikipedia, "Meccan surah"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meccan_surah) separately notes that the German orientalist Theodor Nöldeke's academic reconstruction (*Geschichte des Korans*) diverges from the traditional order on specific surahs (e.g. placement of Surah 110 and Surah 62) — an academic-vs-traditional divergence, not a Sunni-vs-Shia one.
- **Whether the chronological/Mushaf order is divinely dictated (tawqifi) or partly based on companion reasoning (ijtihadi) is an open scholarly question *within* both Sunni and Shia scholarship**, not a clean sectarian split. [Dar al-Ifta, "The difference between the order of revelation and the order of the Mushaf"](https://www.dar-alifta.org/en/fatwa/details/22355/the-difference-between-the-order-of-revelation-and-the-order-of-the-mushaf): "The majority of scholars are of the view that both... are tawqifi... Other scholars, however, hold that the order of revelation is entirely tawqifi [while Mushaf arrangement is partly ijtihad]." A dedicated comparative study, [Qom University, "A Survey of Sunni and Shi'ite Commentators' Views about the Chronological Order of the Qur'anic Chapters — Toqifi or Ijtihadi"](https://ptt.qom.ac.ir/article_1538_d20fd9eccaf991bad25b6024447e6bd4.pdf?lang=en), confirms this tawqifi/ijtihadi debate exists among named scholars on *both* sides (citing e.g. Sayyid Murtaza and Ayatollah Khoyi, both Shia, holding the tawqifi view) — i.e. it is a cross-cutting methodological debate, not evidence of a Sunni-vs-Shia disagreement on the actual chronology itself.
- **Meccan/Medinan classification of specific individual verses/surahs** (as opposed to the broad periodization) has disputed cases on both the Sunni and Shia side. [Wikishia](https://en.wikishia.net/view/Meccan_and_Medinan_Suras) states plainly of the 114 surahs: "It is agreed that the Madani suras are 20, the disputed cases are 12, and the rest are Makki" — i.e. 12 surahs have contested classification even by Shia scholarship's own accounting, independent of any Sunni-Shia split.

## 5. Sunni–Shia disagreement: where it actually is (and isn't)

No source found in this research identifies a Sunni–Shia disagreement specifically on **(a)** the Muhammad→Adnan lineage chain, or **(b)** the basic 23-year/13+10-year/Hijra-pivot chronology shape. The Wikishia source, a Shia reference encyclopedia, describes the Meccan/Medinan classification criteria in essentially the same terms as the Sunni sources surveyed.

Where a real, sourced Sunni–Shia divergence does exist (relevant context for the broader "lineage/history diagrams" pillar, even if not the lineage chain itself):
- **History of the Quran's compilation.** [The Jenkins Center (Southern Baptist Theological Seminary's apologetics center — not an Islamic source; cited here only for its documentation of the historical Shia positions, sourced in turn to the academic work of Hossein Modarressi), "The Falsification of the Qur'an"](https://jenkins.sbts.edu/blog/the-falsification-of-the-quran/): early/pre-Buwayhid Shia sources held that Ali possessed a fuller version of the revelation than the Uthmanic codex, which was allegedly suppressed; however, the same source documents that this position was superseded within mainstream Twelver Shia scholarship by the 10th century — quoting Ibn Babawayh (d. 991): *"Our belief is that the Qur'an... is (the same as) the one between the two boards [the Uthmanic version]... and he who asserts that it is greater in extent than this is a liar."* **This should be treated as a historical-debate footnote, not a live doctrinal split** — mainstream Sunni and Shia scholarship today both affirm the standard Uthmanic text. Given the source's non-Islamic provenance, any use of this specific claim in project content should be corroborated against a Muslim-authored source before publication.
- **Revelation-order methodology (tawqifi/ijtihadi)** — see §4 above; this cuts across both traditions rather than dividing them.

## 6. Existing free/open diagram or chart resources (as cross-checks, not sole sources)

No canonical, single "official" open diagram from a major seminary or standards body (e.g. Al-Azhar itself, a national fatwa council) was found for either the lineage or the revelation chronology. What exists:

- [Islamic Academy of Coventry / Islamic Portal (UK), "Seerah Series Part 2: The Lineage" (free PDF)](https://islamicportal.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2018/01/Seerah-Series-Part-2-The-Lineage-II-2nd-Edition.pdf) — a structured, citation-bearing free educational booklet covering 'Abd al-Muttalib's children and the Prophet's ﷺ immediate family; does not itself extend the diagram past Adnan. The same publisher's ["Prophetic lineage to Prophet Adam"](https://islamicportal.co.uk/prophetic-lineage-to-prophet-adam/) page is the strongest free source found that states the pre-Adnan caveat explicitly and cites primary Arabic texts (al-Dhahabi's *Siyar*) — useful as a direct reference for how to caveat a diagram, not just as a chart.
- [Al Qamar Publications, "Family Tree of Nabi SAW" (free downloadable image/PDF)](https://www.alqamarpublications.com/family-tree-of-nabi-saw/) — a grassroots Islamic publisher's free chart; no citation trail was visible in the search results, so it should not be treated as authoritative on its own, only as a visual-layout reference.
- [Wikipedia, "Family tree of Muhammad" (Shaiba ibn Hashim)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_tree_of_Shaiba_ibn_Hashim) — sourced, CC-licensed, and explicitly states the Muhammad→Adnan chain is "21 generations" of "patrilineal ancestors," matching the Bukhari-derived chain in §1; useful as a cross-check for the settled segment, and notable for stopping at Adnan in its main genealogy table (it separately, and distinctly, discusses a "family tree linking prophets to Shi'ite Imams" as its own labeled section — i.e. Wikipedia itself keeps the two lineages, and the certain/uncertain segments, visually and structurally separate, which is a good precedent for how this project's diagram might handle the same distinction).
- For revelation chronology, [Tanzil.net's Revelation Order documentation](https://tanzil.net/docs/revelation_order) is the most usable free, structured, tabular resource (full 114-surah chronological table with per-surah notes on mixed-origin verses), sourced to al-Zanjani's *The History of the Quran*, with an explicit note on where Nöldeke's Western academic ordering diverges. This is a reasonable structural reference for a "revelation timeline" diagram, provided the "this is the traditional/Ibn Abbas order, not the only one proposed" caveat from §4 travels with it.

## Recommendation for the diagram ticket (not a decision — for the eventual implementer + human reviewer to weigh)

1. Depict Muhammad ﷺ → Adnan as a settled, single-line genealogy (cite Sahih al-Bukhari + the consensus framing in §1).
2. Either omit the Adnan → Ibrahim segment, or render it visually distinct (e.g. dashed/lighter, with an inline footnote citing §2) — do not present one specific list of names as the settled answer.
3. Depict the 23-year / 13+10-year / Hijra-pivot revelation timeline as settled, with Hira cave and the Hijra as anchor milestones.
4. Avoid stating a single exact date for the first revelation; if a date is shown, caveat it (§4) or omit day-level precision and show "Ramadan, ~610 CE" only.
5. If a surah-by-surah chronological order is shown, label it explicitly as "traditional order (Ibn 'Abbas tradition, per the 1924 standard Mushaf)" rather than presenting it as the unique historical fact, and do not use it to derive precise Meccan/Medinan surah counts (§3).
6. Final content — wording, imagery, and especially anything implying certainty beyond what §2/§4 support — needs sign-off from a qualified human Islamic-studies/history reviewer before publication, per the map issue's standing constraint. This research file is not that sign-off.
